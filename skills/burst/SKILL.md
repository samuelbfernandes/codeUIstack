# Burst: three independent versions, graded

A first draft is the safest, most average guess. Three drafts made without seeing each other, then graded, usually
contain one worth keeping. It costs about three times a single draft, so use it for short, high-value text and not for big work.

## Steps
1. **State the brief in four lines**: what is being made, who reads it, the goal, what done looks like. If the person
   left one out, ask once.
2. **Make three versions that differ in approach**, not in wording (for example direct, story-led, question-led). Where
   subagents are available, give each its own subagent with only the brief, so no version sees another; otherwise write
   them one after the other and do not borrow phrases between them. Prefer a smaller model for the subagents.
3. **Grade each against the goal**, with one line per criterion that matters (clarity, fit to the reader, tone, length).
   Be willing to say none is good.
4. **Recommend one** and say why in two lines. Offer to merge the best parts of the others, and do not do it unprompted.
5. **Say the cost**: "3 versions, about 3x the tokens of one."

## Notes
- Skip it for code, long documents and anything where one correct answer exists.
- Keep every version complete enough to be used as is.
